RT and PEG feeding

When a patient cannot eat enough by mouth, tube feeding can give them the nutrition they need. MedMeal makes feeds for both tube types.

RT / Ryles tube

  • 01

    What it is: A Ryles tube, also called a nasogastric (NG) tube, is a thin, soft tube passed through the nose into the stomach. No procedure is needed, so feeding can start the same day.

  • 02

    When it's used: Usually the first tube tried when a patient suddenly can't eat or drink enough by mouth, for example after a stroke, a head injury, or major surgery.

  • 03

    How long: Generally a few weeks. If longer-term feeding is needed, doctors usually switch to a PEG tube instead.

PEG tube

  • 01

    What it is: A PEG tube (percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy) is placed directly through the skin of the abdomen into the stomach, in a short day procedure done by a doctor.

  • 02

    When it's used: For longer-term feeding, weeks, months, or longer, and it's generally more comfortable than a nose tube for long-term use.

  • 03

    The outcome: Patients who start PEG feeding after a period of weight loss steadily regain weight over the following months, because they're reliably getting the nutrition they need every day.

Why proper nutrition matters

Missing nutrition has real risks. Here's how MedMeal keeps it on track.

Why it matters

Going too long without enough food can cause weight and strength loss, or a serious lung infection if food reaches the lungs instead of the stomach.
